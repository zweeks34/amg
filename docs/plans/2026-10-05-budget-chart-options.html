AMG · Budget

Month by month, by type

Four ways to show what each month's maintenance goes on. The figures are a test Outback's, made up to try these out, not your car's. Hover over or tap a column or dot to see its jobs, and switch to 3 years to see how each copes with 36 months.

A

Stacked columns

Today's chart with each month's column split by type. Each type keeps one colour everywhere in AMG; only Engine, Brakes, and Tires and wheels get a colour, and everything else shares grey.

    Good for

    • The total and what makes it up, in one look
    • Closest to the chart you have now

    Watch out for

    • Only three colours stay easy to tell apart for colour-blind readers and in dark mode, so the rest share grey. Here, wipers, fees and the cabin filter come to $221 of grey, more than the brakes
    • Thin slivers at 3 years
    B

    Pick a type

    Today's chart, with the types listed above it. Pick one and its part of each month is highlighted; pick "All types" for the chart as it is now.

    Good for

    • One colour, so any number of types works
    • Fine at 3 years and on a phone
    • The By type list on the page can do the picking

    Watch out for

    • One type at a time
    • Until you pick a type, it's today's chart
    C

    A strip per type

    A row of small month bars for each type, every row on the same scale, most expensive type first. It sits under today's chart, which keeps the month totals and the put-aside tape.

    Good for

    • Seeing when each type lands, with no colours and no tapping
    • Any number of types

    Watch out for

    • Taller: about one line per type
    • An addition to today's chart, not a replacement
    D

    Type by month grid

    Types down, months across, with a dot sized by what that month's work costs and each type's total at the end of its row.

    Good for

    • The most compact overview: quiet months and big ones stand out
    • Row totals beside the timing

    Watch out for

    • Dot sizes are rough; the amount is in the hover
    • 36 columns scroll sideways on a phone

    My pick: B, with C as a close second

    Your budget is lumpy: most months have no jobs or one, and a car has six to ten types over a few years. That suits a chart that shows one type clearly rather than all of them at once. B keeps the chart you know, needs no new colours, and still works at 3 years and on a phone. If you'd rather see every type without picking, C does that, at the cost of some height.

    A is what most apps do, and it reads well when one or two types dominate. With three colours plus grey, though, "Other" is often one of the biggest parts. A mix works too: A's colours for the three big types, plus B's picker to bring any type forward.

    On every option, the month list under the chart still names each job and its price, so nothing depends on telling colours or sizes apart.
